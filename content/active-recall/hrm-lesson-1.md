
# Chapter 1 — Review Questions, Answers & Short Examples

## A. HRM and Human Capital

### 1. What is Human Resource Management?

**Answer:**
HRM is the **policies, practices, and systems that influence employees’ behavior, attitudes, and performance**.

**Example:** A company changes its training and reward systems to improve how employees work.

---

### 2. Who is responsible for HRM in an organization?

**Answer:**
HRM is not only the responsibility of the HR department. **HR professionals, managers, supervisors, and top management** all have HR responsibilities.

**Example:** HR designs a performance system, while supervisors use it to evaluate their employees.

---

### 3. What is human capital, and what are its six elements?

**Answer:**
Human capital describes employees in terms of their **training, experience, judgment, intelligence, relationships, and insight**.

**Example:** An experienced manager may bring strong judgment, useful relationships, and industry experience.

---

### 4. What is the difference between the type of human capital and the behavior of human capital?

**Answer:**

* **Type of human capital:** qualities employees possess.
* **Behavior of human capital:** mainly their **motivation and effort**.

**Example:** An employee may have strong experience (**type**) but put little effort into the job (**behavior**).

---

### 5. How does HRM contribute to organizational performance?

**Answer:**
HRM influences both the **type of human capital** and **how employees behave**. These can contribute to **quality, profitability, and customer satisfaction**.

**Example:** Selecting capable employees and motivating them to work well can help improve customer service.

---

### 6. Give one example of how HRM can affect the type of human capital and one example of how it can affect human-capital behavior.

**Answer:**

* HRM affects **type** by influencing who joins the organization.
* HRM affects **behavior** by influencing employees’ motivation and effort.

**Example:** Selection brings in a skilled employee; a performance bonus may encourage greater effort.

---

### 7. Why can employees be an important source of sustainable competitive advantage?

**Answer:**
Human resources can be:

* **valuable**;
* **rare**;
* **cannot be imitated**;
* **have no good substitutes**.

**Example:** A highly talented and experienced manager may be difficult for competitors to find and reproduce.

These four characteristics use the terminology of the Chapter 1 slide/textbook.

---

# B. Major HRM Practices

Chapter 1 identifies eight major practices and explains that they should support business strategy and be managed well together.

### 8. What are the eight major HRM practices?

**Answer:**

1. Analysis and Design of Work
2. HR Planning
3. Recruiting
4. Selection
5. Training and Development
6. Performance Management
7. Compensation
8. Employee Relations

**Example:** Hiring a new employee may involve several practices, such as analyzing the job, recruiting candidates, selecting one, and then training the new hire.

---

### 9. What is analysis and design of work?

**Answer:**
It involves understanding **what work needs to be done and how jobs and tasks should be organized**.

**Example:** A supermarket identifies the duties and skills required for a cashier job.

---

### 10. What is the purpose of HR planning?

**Answer:**
To identify **how many employees and what kinds of employees the organization will need**.

**Example:** A hotel estimates that it will need 20 more housekeepers next summer.

---

### 11. What is the difference between recruitment and selection?

**Answer:**
**Recruitment attracts applicants; selection chooses suitable applicants.**

**Example:** Posting a vacancy online is recruitment; interviewing applicants and choosing one is selection.

---

### 12. What is the difference between training and development?

**Answer:**
Training mainly prepares employees for their **current jobs**.
Development prepares employees for **future responsibilities and changes**.

**Example:** Learning new software for the current job is training; mentoring for a future management role is development.

---

### 13. What is performance management?

**Answer:**
Performance management ensures that employees’ **activities and outputs contribute to organizational goals**.

**Example:** A sales manager sets sales targets, reviews results, and gives employees feedback.

---

### 14. What is compensation? Give two examples.

**Answer:**
Compensation refers to the rewards employees receive for their work.

Examples include:

* salary or wages;
* bonuses;
* benefits.

**Example:** A salesperson receives a basic salary plus a sales commission.

---

### 15. What is employee relations?

**Answer:**
Employee relations focuses on maintaining a **positive relationship between employees and the organization**.

**Example:** HR receives an employee complaint and works with the people involved to resolve the issue.

---

### 16. Should the eight HRM practices be treated as a strict step-by-step process? Why?

**Answer:**
No. They are **interconnected practices** that should support business strategy and work effectively together.

**Example:** Hiring excellent employees is not enough if the company does not train, manage, and reward them effectively.

---

# C. HR Department — Three Product Lines

The textbook presents these as **three complementary product lines**, not three stages that replace one another.

### 17. What are the three product lines of an HR department?

**Answer:**

1. **Administrative services and transactions**
2. **Business partner services**
3. **Strategic partner**

**Example:** The same HR department may process employee benefits, improve recruitment systems, and advise management about future workforce needs.

---

### 18. What does HR do in administrative services and transactions?

**Answer:**
HR handles necessary administrative tasks **efficiently and accurately**.

**Example:** HR processes a new employee’s hiring information or answers a question about benefits.

---

### 19. What does HR do as a business partner?

**Answer:**
HR develops effective HR systems that help the organization **attract, keep, and develop people with the skills it needs**.

**Example:** A company needs more skilled technicians, so HR improves its recruiting and training systems.

---

### 20. What does HR do as a strategic partner?

**Answer:**
HR contributes to company strategy by understanding its **existing and future human resources** and how HR practices can contribute to competitive advantage.

**Example:** A company plans to use new technology, so HR identifies the new skills the workforce will need.

---

### 21. What must HR understand to work effectively as a business and strategic partner?

**Answer:**
HR must understand the **business**. For strategic contribution, HR also needs to understand its **industry, competitors, and human-resource needs**.

**Example:** Before recommending a new hiring strategy, HR needs to understand what skills the company actually needs to compete.

---

### 22. Can an HR department perform all three product lines at the same time?

**Answer:**
**Yes.** They are complementary roles, not three stages that replace one another.

**Example:** HR can process payroll-related information today while also developing a talent system and advising management about future workforce needs.

The three product lines and their requirements are stated directly in Chapter 1.

# D. Case — Predictive Scheduling

Phần này **không cần thêm example**, vì chính case đã là example/application context.

### 23. Why do restaurants in the case change employees’ schedules at short notice?

**Answer:**
To match staffing with **changing customer demand** and reduce unnecessary labor costs.

---

### 24. What major problems can unpredictable schedules create for employees?

**Answer:**
They can make it difficult to:

* take a second job;
* attend school;
* arrange childcare;
* get enough sleep and rest.

---

### 25. What do predictive-scheduling laws try to change, and what may happen if employers give too little notice?

**Answer:**
They aim to give employees more **advance notice and schedule predictability**.

With too little notice, employers may have to provide **extra compensation**.

---

### 26. How does Mary Cho approach employee scheduling?

**Answer:**
She asks employees **when they are available** and schedules them during times they have not blocked off.

---

# E. Case — Understanding and Application

### 27. Is the scheduling system in the case really “flexible”? Flexible for whom?

**Answer:**
It is flexible mainly for the **employer**, because the restaurant can quickly adjust staffing to customer demand.

For employees, unpredictable schedules may actually reduce their ability to organize the rest of their lives.

---

### 28. What is the main ethical and business trade-off in this case?

**Answer:**
The restaurant wants **efficiency and staffing flexibility**, while employees need **predictability, adequate rest, and time for responsibilities outside work**.

A better scheduling system should try to balance **business needs and fair treatment of employees**.
