# A. Work Flow Design & Work Flow Analysis

### 1. What is work flow design?

**Answer:** It is the process of analyzing the tasks needed to produce a product or service and assigning them to jobs and positions.
**Short example:** A café identifies the tasks of taking orders, making drinks, and serving customers, then assigns them to different jobs.

### 2. What is the difference between a job and a position?

**Answer:** A **job** is a set of related duties. A **position** is those duties performed by one particular person.
**Short example:** “Lecturer” is a job; three lecturers doing that job represent three positions.

### 3. What are the three main elements of work flow analysis?

**Answer:** **Outputs → Activities / work processes → Inputs.**
**Short example:** A restaurant analyzes the meal it must provide → cooking activities → ingredients, kitchen equipment, and employee skills.

### 4. What is an output? Can it be intangible?

**Answer:** An output is the product, information, or service produced by a work unit. Yes, it can be tangible or intangible.
**Short example:** A manufactured table is tangible; customer service is intangible.

### 5. What are work processes or activities?

**Answer:** They are the tasks employees perform to produce the required output.
**Short example:** To deliver an online order, workers may pick, pack, and ship the product.

### 6. What are the three main types of inputs?

**Answer:** **Materials and information, equipment, and human resources.**
**Short example:** A bakery needs flour, an oven, and bakers with the required knowledge and skills.

### 7. Why does work flow analysis normally start with the output?

**Answer:** Because managers first need to know **what must be produced** before deciding what activities and resources are needed.
**Short example:** A hotel first defines the required output as a clean guest room, then identifies cleaning tasks and required resources.

# B. Work Flow & Organizational Structure

### 8. What is the difference between centralized and decentralized structures?

**Answer:** Centralized structures concentrate authority near the top; decentralized structures distribute authority among more people.
**Short example:** In one company, head office approves most decisions; in another, branch managers can make many decisions themselves.

### 9. What is the difference between functional and divisional grouping?

**Answer:** Functional grouping organizes jobs by specialized functions; divisional grouping organizes them around products, customers, or other broader outputs.
**Short example:** A company may have separate Marketing and Finance departments, or separate divisions for Product A and Product B.

### 10. How can organizational structure affect jobs?

**Answer:** Functional structures tend to support more specialized jobs, while broader or divisional structures tend to support teamwork and broader responsibility.
**Short example:** In a functional structure, a marketer may focus only on marketing; in a product division, employees from different functions may work together for one product.

# C. Job Analysis, Job Description & Job Specification

### 11. What is job analysis?

**Answer:** It is the process of getting detailed information about jobs.
**Short example:** A company studies what cashiers do and what qualifications they need.

### 12. What two basic questions does job analysis answer?

**Answer:** **What does the job involve?** and **What does a person need to perform it successfully?**
**Short example:** For a barista: What tasks does the barista perform? What knowledge and skills are needed?

### 13. What are the two main outputs of job analysis?

**Answer:** **Job description** and **job specification.**
**Short example:** For a receptionist, one document describes the duties and another identifies the qualifications needed.

### 14. What is a job description, and what does TDR mean?

**Answer:** A job description describes the work involved. **TDR = Tasks, Duties, and Responsibilities.**
**Short example:** A receptionist's TDRs may include answering calls and scheduling appointments.

### 15. What is a job specification, and what does KSAO mean?

**Answer:** A job specification describes what a person needs for the job. **KSAO = Knowledge, Skills, Abilities, and Other characteristics.**
**Short example:** A receptionist may need communication skills and knowledge of the booking system.

### 16. What is the main difference between TDRs and KSAOs?

**Answer:** **TDRs describe the work; KSAOs describe what the person needs to perform the work.**
**Short example:** “Prepare monthly reports” is a TDR; “Excel skill” may be a KSAO needed to perform it.

### 17. What is the difference between knowledge, skill, and ability?

**Answer:** **Knowledge** is information a person knows; **skill** is proficiency in performing a task; **ability** is a more general and enduring capability.
**Short example:** A driver may know traffic rules, be skilled at operating a vehicle, and have good spatial-orientation ability.

### 18. Why should job descriptions and specifications be reviewed over time?

**Answer:** Because jobs and their requirements can change.
**Short example:** After a company introduces new software, an accountant may need to perform new tasks and use new skills.

# D. Why Job Analysis Matters Across HRM

### 19. Why is job analysis considered a foundation of HRM?

**Answer:** Because many HR decisions depend on knowing what jobs involve and what employees need to perform them.
**Short example:** Information about a nurse's job can be used when hiring, training, and evaluating nurses.

### 20. How does job analysis support HR planning and selection?

**Answer:** HR planning uses it to identify needed human resources; selection uses it to identify the KSAOs candidates should possess.
**Short example:** If a company needs more data analysts, job analysis helps identify both how many are needed and what skills candidates should have.

### 21. How does job analysis support training and career planning?

**Answer:** Training uses it to identify what employees need to learn; career planning uses it to understand the requirements of different jobs.
**Short example:** A junior accountant may train in financial analysis because that skill is required for a higher-level accounting job.

### 22. How does job analysis support performance appraisal and job evaluation?

**Answer:** Performance appraisal uses job requirements to evaluate performance; job evaluation uses job information to compare jobs for pay purposes.
**Short example:** A supervisor can evaluate whether a receptionist performs required duties, while HR can compare that job's responsibilities with other jobs.

### 23. How does job analysis support work redesign?

**Answer:** Managers need to understand the current work before deciding how it should be changed.
**Short example:** Before transferring some tasks from doctors to nurses, a hospital first needs to understand what each job currently involves.

# E. Sources of Job Information

### 24. What are the main sources of information for job analysis?

**Answer:** Incumbents, supervisors or observers, trained job analysts, objective data, and occupational information sources.
**Short example:** HR may interview a cashier, talk to the supervisor, and examine system records about the work.

### 25. What is an incumbent, and why is an incumbent useful?

**Answer:** An incumbent is a person currently performing the job and therefore knows its day-to-day activities.
**Short example:** A current hotel receptionist can explain what actually happens during the check-in process.

### 26. Why should job analysts use more than one source?

**Answer:** Different sources may provide different perspectives, giving a more complete picture of the job.
**Short example:** An employee may know how much time tasks actually take, while a supervisor may know which duties are most important.

# F. Competency Models

### 27. What is a competency model?

**Answer:** It identifies and describes the capabilities needed for successful performance in a job, group of jobs, or organization.
**Short example:** A project-manager competency model may include planning, communication, and problem-solving competencies.

### 28. What is the main difference between traditional job analysis and competency models?

**Answer:** Job analysis focuses more on **tasks and work outcomes**; competency models focus more on **how people need to work successfully**.
**Short example:** “Prepare a project schedule” describes a task; “effective planning” describes a competency.

---

# G. Job Design & Job Redesign

### 29. What is the difference between job design and job redesign?

**Answer:** Job design defines how a job should be organized; job redesign changes an existing job.
**Short example:** Creating a new customer-service job is job design; adding online-chat duties to an existing customer-service job is job redesign.

### 30. What should managers understand before designing or redesigning a job?

**Answer:** They should understand both the job itself and how it fits into the larger work flow.
**Short example:** Before redesigning a warehouse worker's job, managers should understand both the worker's tasks and the whole order-fulfilment process.

### 31. What are the four major approaches to job design?

**Answer:** **Efficiency, motivation, safety and health, and mental capacity.**
**Short example:** A packaging job can be designed to reduce wasted movement, remain motivating, prevent physical strain, and avoid unnecessary mental overload.

# H. Designing for Efficiency & Motivation

### 32. What is the main goal of the industrial engineering approach?

**Answer:** To find a simple and efficient way to perform the work.
**Short example:** Tools can be placed close to workers so they make fewer unnecessary movements.

### 33. What is a possible problem with designing a job only for efficiency?

**Answer:** The job may become too repetitive and less motivating.
**Short example:** An assembly worker who performs the same simple action all day may become bored.

### 34. What are the five characteristics in the Job Characteristics Model?

**Answer:** **Skill variety, task identity, task significance, autonomy, and feedback.**
**Short example:** A technician who diagnoses a problem, completes the repair, chooses how to do it, and sees whether the machine works experiences several of these characteristics.

### 35. How can these five job characteristics affect motivation?

**Answer:** Jobs with more of these characteristics tend to have greater motivational potential, although employees may respond differently.
**Short example:** Giving a technician more freedom to choose the repair method may increase autonomy and make the job more motivating.

### 36. What is the difference between job enlargement and job enrichment?

**Answer:** **Job enlargement broadens tasks; job enrichment increases responsibility and decision-making authority.**
**Short example:** A cashier who also restocks shelves has job enlargement; a cashier allowed to solve some customer problems without manager approval has job enrichment.

### 37. What is job rotation?

**Answer:** It means moving employees among different jobs over time.
**Short example:** A hotel employee may rotate between reception, reservations, and customer-service jobs.

### 38. How can self-managing teams make work more motivating?

**Answer:** They can increase autonomy, skill variety, and responsibility for a meaningful part of the work process.
**Short example:** A production team may organize its own work schedule and divide tasks among team members.

# I. Designing for Human Capabilities

### 39. What is ergonomics, and what is its main purpose?

**Answer:** Ergonomics studies the fit between people and the physical work environment to reduce physical strain and risk.
**Short example:** An adjustable chair and properly positioned monitor can reduce physical strain for an office worker.

### 40. What is the main purpose of designing jobs for mental capacity?

**Answer:** To help employees process necessary information accurately without unnecessary mental overload.
**Short example:** A control dashboard can highlight only the information that requires immediate attention.

### 41. Give two ways an organization can reduce unnecessary mental demands.

**Answer:** For example, filter unnecessary information, provide clear displays, use simple instructions, or provide memory aids.
**Short example:** A warehouse scanner can show workers the next product and its location instead of requiring them to remember a long list.
